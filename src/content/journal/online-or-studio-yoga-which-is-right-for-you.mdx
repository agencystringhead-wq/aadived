You've decided to start yoga, and now there's a second question: do you roll out a mat in a studio, or open a laptop at home? Both can work well. The right choice depends less on which is "better" and more on your week, your space and what you want from a class.

This guide compares the two honestly, and ends with a quick checklist so you can decide.

## Studio yoga: the strengths

**Hands-on, in-person guidance.** A teacher in the room can see your alignment from every angle and offer a gentle adjustment or a prop. That can help a lot if you're new, or if you're managing a stiff area of your body.

**A space set apart.** You step out of your usual routine and into a room designed for quiet. For many people, travelling to the studio is part of the ritual that tells the mind it's time to slow down.

**Fewer distractions.** No laundry basket, no delivery bell, no laptop notifications. When you're away from home, it's easier to give the practice your full attention.

**Community.** Sharing a room with other people can be motivating, and some enjoy the quiet company even when no one is talking.

**Props within reach.** Studios often keep blocks, bolsters and straps on hand. Ask us what's available before you buy anything.

## Studio yoga: the trade-offs

- **Travel time.** Even a short trip adds time, and Hinjawadi traffic can be unpredictable.
- **Set timings.** You need to be there when the class is on.
- **Less flexibility on tired days.** It's harder to join in from your pyjamas on a day you'd rather stay home.

## Live online yoga: the strengths

**Convenience.** There's no commute. If you've got a spare thirty minutes before work, you can practise without changing out of comfortable clothes first.

**Practise anywhere.** Travelling for work, living far from the studio or staying with family? A live class travels with you.

**Privacy.** Some people are more relaxed with the camera angled just how they like it, or even off, with the teacher giving verbal guidance. That can be a gentle way to start.

**Live, not recorded.** In a live class, your teacher can hear your questions and talk you through adjustments as you go, which is the main advantage over a pre-recorded video.

## Live online yoga: the trade-offs

- **You need a little space.** Enough floor to lie down and stretch your arms out.
- **A reliable connection.** A patchy signal can break your concentration.
- **Home distractions.** It's easier to get pulled away by a phone call or a knock at the door.
- **Less physical adjustment.** The teacher can't use touch, so the guidance relies on words and what they can see on screen.

## Which is better for beginners?

Either can work for a beginner. Some people prefer to start in the studio, because the teacher can see and help directly. Others prefer to start at home, where nobody's watching and the mat is already laid out. If you're nervous, a one-to-one intro session can be a gentle way to begin in either format, since the teacher can match the practice to you. ## A quick checklist

Answer each statement honestly.

1. **I live or work close to the studio.** If yes, the studio is easy to reach.
2. **I have a quiet corner at home.** If yes, online is a good option.
3. **I want hands-on correction.** If yes, lean towards the studio.
4. **My schedule changes week to week.** If yes, online may be easier to keep up.
5. **I find it hard to switch off at home.** If yes, the studio's separate space may help.
6. **I'm travelling a lot.** If yes, online keeps your practice going.
7. **I'd feel more motivated around other people.** If yes, try the studio.

If most of your yeses are on the odd numbers, the studio might suit you. If most are on the even numbers, online might. Mixed answers are a good sign that you could try both.

## You don't have to choose just one

Many people mix the two: the studio on days they can make the trip, and a live online class on days they can't. That kind of flexibility often helps people stay consistent, which matters more than any single class. ## Tips for getting the most from online classes

- **Set up your space beforehand.** Clear a patch of floor, and lay out your mat and a cushion.
- **Test your camera and sound.** A few minutes early avoids last-minute stress.
- **Position your device so the teacher can see you.** A laptop on a stable surface works well.
- **Tell the teacher about any injuries before you start.** They can offer other options.
- **Let others at home know.** A quick word saves interruptions.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need any equipment for online classes?

A yoga mat, a clear patch of floor and a device with a camera and speakers are the basics. A cushion or folded blanket can be useful, and you can improvise props from things at home.

### Will the teacher see me during an online class?

In a live class, the teacher can see you if your camera is on, which allows them to give feedback. If you'd rather keep your camera off at first, tell the teacher and they can guide you with spoken instructions.

### Can I switch between studio and online?

You can ask us. Many people move between the two as life changes. Call or message us and we'll help you work out what fits.

Pick the format that gets you onto the mat most often. You can always change your mind, and your practice will go with you.
