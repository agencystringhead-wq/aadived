Mornings are crowded. There's the alarm, the tea, the commute and a laptop waiting for you. But twenty minutes on a mat before the day begins can change how the rest of it feels, and it's easier to protect than you might think.

This routine is gentle, suits most beginners and needs only a mat and some floor space. Read it once, then follow it step by step.

<Note title="Before you begin">

Move slowly and stay within a comfortable range. If you have an injury, a health condition or you're pregnant, please speak to your doctor before starting. This article is for general information.

</Note>

## Why morning practice works for many people

Practising in the morning has a few simple advantages.

- **Fewer interruptions.** Nobody is messaging you at 6:30 am, and your day's demands haven't taken over yet.
- **A natural anchor.** Pairing the routine with something you already do, such as waking up, makes it easier to remember.
- **A calmer start.** Many people find that slow movement and steady breath set a steadier tone for the hours that follow.
- **Warming up a stiff body.** After a night's sleep, gentle movement helps you feel looser.

If you're not a morning person, you can still use this routine at another time of day. The best time is the one you'll keep.

## What you'll need

- A yoga mat or a folded blanket on a non-slip floor
- Comfortable clothes you can move in
- A glass of water nearby
- A timer, if you like (twenty minutes in total)
- A phone on silent, face down

Practise on an empty or light stomach. A big breakfast before a forward fold isn't comfortable, so eat afterwards.

## The 20-minute routine

Each step lists the time and what to do. Move slowly, and breathe through your nose.

1. **Arrive (2 minutes).** Sit comfortably, close your eyes and take slow breaths. Notice how your body feels this morning, without judging it. Let each out-breath be a little longer than the in-breath.
2. **Cat-cow (2 minutes).** Come onto hands and knees. Breathe in as you lift your chest and look slightly up. Breathe out as you round your back. Move slowly for about ten breaths.
3. **Child's pose (1 minute).** Sit back towards your heels, fold forward and rest your forehead on the mat. Take five slow breaths. Place a cushion under your forehead or hips if you need to.
4. **Downward-facing dog (2 minutes).** Tuck your toes and lift your hips up and back. Bend your knees as much as you like, and press the floor away with your hands. Breathe for five to eight breaths, then rest in child's pose.
5. **Low lunge, both sides (2 minutes).** Step your right foot forward between your hands, lower your back knee and lift your chest. Hold for five breaths, then switch sides.
6. **Standing forward fold (1 minute).** Stand with your feet hip-width apart and fold forward with soft knees. Let your head and arms hang heavy. Take five breaths. Rise slowly.
7. **Mountain pose with arm lifts (1 minute).** Stand tall. Breathe in as you reach your arms overhead and breathe out as you lower them. Repeat five times.
8. **Warrior II, both sides (3 minutes).** Step your feet wide, turn your right foot out and bend your right knee over your ankle. Reach your arms long and look over your front hand. Hold for five breaths, then switch sides.
9. **Seated twist, both sides (2 minutes).** Sit with your legs crossed. Place your right hand behind you and your left hand on your right knee, and turn gently to the right. Hold for five breaths, then switch.
10. **Lying rest (4 minutes).** Lie on your back with your arms by your sides and your feet falling open. Close your eyes. Let your breathing settle and release the effort of the practice. When the time is up, roll to one side and sit up slowly.

<PullQuote>Twenty minutes you actually do will always beat the hour-long practice you keep postponing.</PullQuote>

## Short on time? Two shorter versions

**The ten-minute version.** Do steps 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 10, trimming the lying rest to two minutes.

**The five-minute version.** Do cat-cow (about eight breaths), a standing forward fold (five breaths) and two minutes of lying rest.

A short practice every day is worth more than a long one now and then.

## Making it stick

A routine only helps if you keep doing it. A few small habits make that easier.

- **Prepare the night before.** Lay your mat out and put your clothes where you'll see them.
- **Link it to a cue.** Practise straight after you brush your teeth, or after your first glass of water.
- **Start smaller than you think.** If twenty minutes feels like a stretch, begin with five and build up.
- **Be kind about missed days.** Skip a day and carry on the next. Don't wait for Monday to start again.
- **Track it lightly.** A tick on a calendar is enough to build momentum.

## Listening to your body

Some mornings your body will feel stiff, and some mornings you'll feel full of energy. Both are fine. Use these cues to adjust.

- **Stiff or tired:** Spend longer in child's pose and cat-cow, and shorten the standing poses.
- **Energetic:** Add a few extra breaths in the lunge and Warrior II.
- **Sore back or knees:** Bend your knees in the forward fold and lunge, and use a folded blanket under your knee.
- **Short of breath:** Slow down, and take breaks in child's pose.

Stop any pose that causes sharp or lasting pain. Take your breath as your guide: if you can't breathe comfortably, you're doing too much.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to be flexible for this routine?

No. Every pose has a gentler option. Bend your knees, use a cushion, or reduce the time you hold each pose. Flexibility builds slowly with regular practice.

### Can I do it after work instead?

Yes. Many people prefer an evening practice. If you do it later in the day, you may like to shorten the standing poses and spend longer in the lying rest.

### How long until I notice a difference?

It varies. Many people notice that they feel a little looser or calmer after just one session, and feel more benefit with regular practice over several weeks. Don't focus on results, just on showing up.
