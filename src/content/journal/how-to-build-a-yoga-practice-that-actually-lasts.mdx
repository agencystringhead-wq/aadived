You bought the mat, you went to the first few classes, and for a couple of weeks you felt great. Then a busy week came along, a class got missed, and the mat ended up behind the door. If that's happened to you, you're in very good company. Almost everyone who starts a new habit stumbles somewhere.

The good news is that a lasting practice isn't about willpower. It's about making yoga easy to start, small enough to survive a bad day and kind enough that you want to come back. Here's how.

## Why practices fade

Most practices don't end with a decision. They fade for ordinary reasons:

- **The plan was too big.** An hour every morning sounds admirable until the third week.
- **It depended on motivation.** Motivation comes and goes, and a habit that relies on it will wobble.
- **One missed day became two,** then a week, then "I'll start again on Monday."
- **It felt like a chore.** If every session feels hard or boring, you'll find reasons to skip it.
- **No clear time or place.** A vague plan to "do yoga sometime" rarely happens.
- **Life changed.** Travel, illness and deadlines break routines.

None of these is a personal failing. They're predictable, which means you can plan for them.

## Seven habits that help

### 1. Start smaller than feels worthwhile

Aim for five to ten minutes a day. It may feel too short to matter, but it's short enough to do on your worst day. You can always do more. What you're building first is the habit of turning up.

### 2. Anchor it to something you already do

Link your practice to a cue that happens every day, such as after you brush your teeth, after your morning tea or when you get home from work. This is far easier than trying to remember at a random time.

### 3. Choose a time and a place

Decide when and where, and write it down. "After tea, in the corner of the living room" is much easier to follow than "sometime this evening." If you can, leave your mat unrolled, or at least in sight.

### 4. Make it easy to begin

Lay out your clothes the night before. Keep your mat, a cushion and a block together. Remove the small obstacles, so that starting takes seconds rather than minutes.

### 5. Plan for your bad days

Decide in advance what a "minimum" practice looks like, such as three slow breaths and one stretch. On days when you can't manage more, do the minimum. It keeps the thread alive.

### 6. Keep a light record

A tick on a wall calendar is enough. Seeing a row of ticks builds momentum, and it shows you that something is happening, even when you can't feel it.

### 7. Make it enjoyable

Pick poses you like and a time you look forward to. Play soft music if it helps. A practice you enjoy is a practice you'll keep.

<PullQuote>Don't aim to be perfect. Aim to come back, every time you drift away.</PullQuote>

## A four-week starter plan

This plan builds gently. Adjust it to your own life.

1. **Week 1: Show up for five minutes.** Do five minutes of cat-cow, child's pose and slow breathing, four days this week. That's all.
2. **Week 2: Add a day, or a few minutes.** Aim for five days, and stretch the practice to eight minutes if it feels easy. Add a standing forward fold.
3. **Week 3: Try a short routine.** Follow a ten-minute sequence, such as part of <PostLink slug="a-20-minute-morning-yoga-routine-before-work">a 20-minute morning routine</PostLink>. Choose a time and stick to it.
4. **Week 4: Look back and adjust.** Notice what's working. Is a morning or an evening better? Do you prefer a class or a home practice? Tweak your plan to match.

After four weeks, you'll have the beginnings of a habit, and you'll know a lot more about what suits you.

## What to do when you miss a day

You will miss days. Everyone does. What matters is what happens next.

- **Don't wait for a fresh start.** There's no need to restart on Monday. Practise today.
- **Make the next session easy.** Return with a shorter practice and build back up.
- **Look for the reason.** Was the time wrong? Was the plan too big? Adjust it.
- **Skip the guilt.** Guilt doesn't build habits. A kind, practical response does.
- **Remember your reasons.** Why did you start? Write it down and keep it somewhere you'll see.

A simple rule many people find helpful is to never miss twice. If you miss one day, make the next one a priority, even if it's only two minutes.

## Different kinds of practice

You don't need to do the same thing every day. A varied week can keep you interested.

- **A short daily practice,** such as ten minutes at home.
- **A weekly class,** which gives you a teacher's guidance and a set appointment.
- **A restful session,** such as meditation or a restorative sequence, on tired days.
- **Breathwork,** which you can do anywhere. <PostLink slug="4-breathwork-techniques-for-a-calmer-workday">4 breathwork techniques for a calmer workday</PostLink> has a few to try.

## Getting support

Doing it alone is hard. These can help.

- **A teacher.** Regular classes give you structure, feedback and someone to answer your questions.
- **A friend.** Practise together, online or in person.
- **A community.** Sharing a space with others can keep you going.
- **A plan.** A teacher can help you shape a routine that fits your goals and schedule.

## Listening to your body

A lasting practice respects your body. Some days you'll feel strong, and some days you'll feel tired. Both are fine.

- **Don't push through pain.** Sharp or lasting pain means stop.
- **Rest when you need to.** Rest is part of a practice.
- **Change with the seasons of your life.** What suits you now may change after a move, a new job or a new baby.
- **Check in with your doctor** if you have a health condition or you're returning after an injury.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does it take to form a habit?

There's no set number. It varies from person to person and from habit to habit. Focus on showing up regularly rather than counting days.

### Is it better to practise daily or a few times a week?

Either can work. A short daily practice builds the habit quickly, while a few longer sessions a week may suit a busy schedule. Choose the one you can keep up.

### What if I lose motivation?

It happens to everyone. Make your practice smaller, change the time or style, or book a class so you have an appointment. Remembering why you started can also help.

Roll out your mat today, even for two minutes. A lasting practice is made of small, ordinary days like this one.
