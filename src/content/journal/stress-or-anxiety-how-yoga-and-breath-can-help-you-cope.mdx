Your chest feels tight, your thoughts are racing and you can't tell whether you're just stressed or something more. People often use "stress" and "anxiety" as if they mean the same thing. They overlap, but they aren't identical, and knowing the difference can help you decide what kind of support you need.

This article explains the two in plain language, offers calming yoga and breath practices that many people find useful, and says clearly when it's time to speak to a professional.

<Note title="Before you read">

This article is for general information. Please speak to your doctor before starting a new practice, especially if you're pregnant or managing a health condition. Yoga and breathwork can support everyday coping, but they aren't a replacement for medical or mental health care.

</Note>

## Stress and anxiety: how they differ

**Stress** is usually a response to something specific and external, such as a deadline, a difficult conversation or a hectic week. It tends to ease when the demand passes.

**Anxiety** is more often a lingering feeling of worry, unease or fear, which can continue even when nothing in particular is wrong. It can feel out of proportion to the situation, and it may be hard to switch off.

Both can show up in similar ways:

- A racing heart or tight chest
- Tense shoulders, jaw or stomach
- Trouble sleeping
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability or feeling on edge
- Restlessness or tiredness

Many people feel both at once, and that's normal. The line isn't always clear, and you don't need to label what you feel before you try to look after yourself. If your feelings are strong, last a long time or interfere with daily life, please speak to a doctor or a mental health professional.

## How yoga and breath may help

Yoga isn't a cure for stress or anxiety, but many people find these practices help them cope day to day.

- **Slow breathing** gives you something to do with a racing body. A longer out-breath is a long-standing practice that many find calming.
- **Gentle movement** releases tension that builds up in the shoulders, jaw and back.
- **Attention to the present** moves your mind away from "what if" and towards "what's here now."
- **A regular routine** gives your day a steady point, which many people find comforting.
- **Rest** is built in, as a way of giving your body time to settle.

None of this works instantly, and not every practice suits every person. Notice what helps you, and keep that.

## Three breath practices to try

### 1. The long exhale

Breathe in gently through your nose for four. Breathe out slowly for six. Repeat for two minutes. If you feel lightheaded, return to normal breathing.

### 2. Hand on the heart

Place one hand on your chest and one on your belly. Breathe slowly and notice the rise and fall under your hands. Let each out-breath soften your shoulders. Stay for one to two minutes.

### 3. Five slow breaths

Sit or stand and take five breaths, a little slower than usual. Count each out-breath. This is a good option when you only have a short moment, such as before a call.

## A calming 15-minute yoga sequence

Use a mat and a cushion. Move slowly. Breathe easily throughout.

1. **Easy seated breathing, 2 minutes.** Long exhale, four in and six out.
2. **Cat-cow, 2 minutes.** Move with your breath, about ten rounds.
3. **Child's pose, 2 minutes.** Rest your forehead on a cushion and let your back widen.
4. **Standing forward fold, 1 minute.** Soft knees, heavy head, slow breath.
5. **Seated forward fold, 2 minutes.** Fold over your legs with a cushion on your thighs.
6. **Reclined twist, 1 minute each side.** Let your knees fall to one side with your shoulders soft.
7. **Legs up the wall, 3 minutes.** Rest your legs against a wall and breathe slowly.

End with a minute lying on your back with your hands on your belly.

## Grounding when feelings rise

When anxious feelings spike, it can help to bring your attention to your senses. Name five things you can see, four things you can feel and three things you can hear. Press your feet into the floor. You'll find more ideas in <PostLink slug="grounding-practices-simple-ways-to-feel-present-again">grounding practices: simple ways to feel present again</PostLink>.

## Habits that support you

- **Keep a regular sleep routine.** A steady bedtime helps.
- **Move every day.** A walk counts.
- **Limit caffeine,** especially late in the day, if you notice it makes you jittery.
- **Take breaks from screens and news.** Switch off for a while each evening.
- **Talk to someone.** A friend, family member or counsellor can help carry the load.
- **Make small plans.** Break big tasks into steps.
- **Ask for help early.** There's no need to wait until things feel unmanageable.

## When to ask for professional help

Yoga and breath can be part of looking after yourself, but they have limits. Please speak to a doctor or mental health professional if:

- Your worry or low mood lasts for weeks, or keeps coming back
- You have panic attacks
- You can't sleep, eat or concentrate as usual
- Your feelings affect your work, studies or relationships
- You feel hopeless, or you have thoughts of harming yourself

If you're in immediate danger or thinking about harming yourself, please contact your local emergency services or a crisis helpline right away. In India, the Tele-MANAS helpline is available on 14416 or 1800-891-4416. You can also speak to someone you trust, now.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can yoga replace therapy or medication?

No. Yoga and breathwork can support everyday coping, but they don't replace therapy, medication or other care your doctor recommends. If you take medication, please keep taking it unless your doctor says otherwise.

### How soon will I feel calmer?

Some people notice a difference after a single practice, such as a few slow breaths. Others find it takes weeks of regular practice. Be patient, and notice what helps.

### What if breathing practices make me feel worse?

Stop and return to normal breathing. Some people feel uneasy when they focus on their breath. Try a movement-based or grounding practice instead, and let your teacher or doctor know.

Be gentle with yourself. Looking after your wellbeing is not a luxury, it's something you're allowed to do every day.
