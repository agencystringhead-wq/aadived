It's 11:40, your inbox is full, a call starts in five minutes, and your shoulders are somewhere near your ears. You can't walk out for an hour of yoga, but you can take a minute to change how you breathe. Of all the tools in a yoga practice, breathwork is the one you can carry into the office.

Here are four simple techniques, each with a clear count and a note on when to use it. You don't need a mat or any special equipment, and nobody around you will notice.

<Note title="Before you begin">

Breathing practices should feel comfortable. If you feel dizzy, light-headed or anxious, stop and breathe normally. Please speak to your doctor before trying breath holds if you're pregnant or have a heart, lung or blood pressure condition. This article is for general information.

</Note>

## Why the breath is a good place to start

Most of us breathe quickly and high in the chest when we're busy, often without noticing. Slowing down and lengthening your out-breath is a long-standing yoga practice, and many people find it helps them feel calmer and more settled. Breath is also always with you, so it's a tool you can use anywhere.

The four techniques below build on each other. Start with the first, and add the others as they feel familiar.

## 1. The long exhale (best for: any time you feel rushed)

This is the simplest technique and a good first one to learn.

1. Sit comfortably with your feet on the floor and your shoulders soft.
2. Breathe in gently through your nose for a count of four.
3. Breathe out slowly through your nose, or through softly pursed lips, for a count of six.
4. Repeat for one to two minutes, about eight to ten rounds.

If six feels too long, try three in and four out. The aim is an out-breath that's a little longer than the in-breath, without any strain.

## 2. Box breathing (best for: before a meeting or presentation)

Box breathing uses four equal sides, like a square, which gives your mind a steady pattern to follow.

1. Breathe out fully.
2. Breathe in through your nose for a count of four.
3. Hold gently for four.
4. Breathe out for four.
5. Hold the empty breath gently for four.
6. Repeat four to six rounds.

Keep each hold light. You shouldn't feel you're gripping or straining. If holding is uncomfortable, simply skip the holds and breathe in for four and out for four. Please skip the holds entirely if you're pregnant or have a health condition that makes them unsuitable.

## 3. Alternate-nostril breathing (best for: a mid-afternoon reset)

Known in yoga as Nadi Shodhana, this practice is a calm, rhythmic way to bring your attention back to the present.

1. Sit tall. Rest your left hand on your lap.
2. Fold the index and middle fingers of your right hand towards your palm. Rest your thumb beside your right nostril and your ring finger beside your left.
3. Close your right nostril with your thumb. Breathe in slowly through the left nostril.
4. Close the left nostril with your ring finger, release the right, and breathe out through the right.
5. Breathe in through the right, close it, release the left, and breathe out through the left.
6. That's one round. Do five to ten rounds, keeping the breath slow and quiet.

Don't force the breath, and don't hold it. If one nostril is blocked, skip this practice for the day and return to the long exhale instead.

## 4. Humming breath (best for: a noisy head at the end of the day)

Humming breath, or Bhramari, uses a soft vibration that many people find soothing. Try it where you won't mind a gentle sound, such as in the car before you leave the car park, or at home.

1. Sit comfortably and close your eyes.
2. Breathe in gently through your nose.
3. Breathe out with your lips closed, making a low humming sound like a bee.
4. Feel the vibration in your face and chest.
5. Repeat five to eight rounds.

Keep the hum soft rather than loud. If you have an ear infection or a sinus problem, skip this one.

## A two-minute desk routine

Here's how to put them together when you only have a short break.

1. **Reset your posture (15 seconds).** Feet flat, spine tall, shoulders dropped.
2. **Long exhale (45 seconds).** Four in, six out, about five rounds.
3. **Box breathing (45 seconds).** Three rounds without holds if you prefer.
4. **Notice (15 seconds).** Check in with your jaw, shoulders and hands. Let each one soften.

Done on most days, this small routine can help you notice stress earlier, and give you a moment to respond more calmly. ## Some good habits

- **Breathe through your nose** unless a technique says otherwise.
- **Keep it quiet and easy.** If you're straining, ease off.
- **Practise when you're calm too.** If you only try these when you're stressed, they'll feel unfamiliar. A minute each morning makes them easier to reach for later.
- **Link them to cues.** Try a long exhale each time you sit down at your desk, or each time you wait for a lift.
- **Stop if it feels wrong.** Dizziness, tingling or a sense of panic means you should go back to normal breathing.

## When to ask for guidance

Breathwork is simple, but a teacher can help you get the counts right, correct tense shoulders and tailor the practice to your body. That's especially useful if you've had trouble sleeping, feel anxious often, or want to go beyond these basics. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do these at my desk?

Yes. The long exhale and box breathing are quiet and take about a minute. Alternate-nostril and humming breath are better done in a private space, as they involve your hands or a sound.

### How often should I practise?

Short, regular practice tends to work better than the occasional long one. A minute or two on most days is a good start.

### Should I breathe through my nose or mouth?

For these four practices, breathe through your nose unless the instructions say otherwise. If your nose is blocked, breathe through your mouth for the long exhale and return to nasal breathing when you can.

Choose one technique this week, and try it before your first meeting tomorrow. A calmer workday often starts with one slower breath.
