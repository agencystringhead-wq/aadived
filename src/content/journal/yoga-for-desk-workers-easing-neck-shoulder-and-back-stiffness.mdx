By mid-afternoon, your neck has gone stiff, your shoulders have crept up, and your lower back feels as if it's been folded into a chair all day. If you work in an IT park, that's the shape of an ordinary Tuesday. The good news is that a few well-chosen stretches, done often, can leave you feeling looser and more upright.

This guide covers why desk work causes that stiffness, eight gentle yoga stretches you can do in or near your chair, and a ten-minute routine to string them together.

## Why desk work makes you stiff

Your body isn't designed to hold one position for eight hours. When you sit at a laptop, your head tends to drift forward, your shoulders roll in, and your hips stay bent for long stretches. Muscles held in the same position for a long time can feel tight and tired, and joints may feel stiff when you finally stand up.

Movement is the simplest answer. Frequent, small changes of position tend to help more than a single long stretch once a day. Yoga is a good fit because it links gentle movement with steady breathing, which many people find helps them let go of tension.

<Note title="Before you start">

Move slowly and stay within a comfortable range. Stretching shouldn't hurt. If you have an injury, numbness, tingling or pain that doesn't ease, please see your doctor before starting a new practice. This article is for general information.

</Note>

## Eight stretches to try

Hold each stretch for three to five slow breaths unless it says otherwise.

### 1. Neck side stretch

Sit tall. Let your right ear drift towards your right shoulder, keeping your left shoulder relaxed. For a slightly deeper stretch, rest your right hand lightly on the side of your head. Don't pull. Switch sides.

### 2. Chin tuck

Sit tall and glide your chin straight back, as if making a gentle double chin, keeping your eyes level. Hold for three breaths and release. Repeat five times. It's a small movement that helps counter the forward head position.

### 3. Shoulder rolls

Lift your shoulders towards your ears as you breathe in. Roll them back and down as you breathe out. Do eight slow rolls, then eight in the other direction.

### 4. Seated cat-cow

Place your hands on your knees. As you breathe in, lift your chest and look slightly up. As you breathe out, round your back and tuck your chin. Move slowly for six to eight breaths.

### 5. Seated eagle arms

Reach your arms forward, cross your right arm under your left, and bend your elbows so your palms or the backs of your hands come together. Lift your elbows slightly and breathe into the space between your shoulder blades. Switch sides. If this feels awkward, simply hug yourself and let your hands rest on your upper back.

### 6. Seated spinal twist

Sit tall, place your right hand on your left knee and your left hand behind you on the chair. As you breathe out, turn gently to the left, leading with your chest. Keep both hips on the chair. Switch sides. Keep the twist comfortable and turn from the upper back, not by wrenching your lower back.

### 7. Standing forward fold with a chair

Stand a step away from your chair and fold forward, resting your hands on the back of the seat. Let your head hang, bend your knees as much as you like, and breathe. This eases the back of your legs and lower back. Come up slowly.

### 8. Chest opener at the wall

Stand beside a wall, place your right palm on it at shoulder height with your fingers pointing back, and turn your body gently away. You should feel a stretch across the chest and front of the shoulder. Switch sides.

<PullQuote>A body that sits all day doesn't need one heroic stretch. It needs small, frequent reminders to move.</PullQuote>

## A ten-minute routine

Try this once a day, perhaps after lunch, or at the end of the day before you leave.

1. **Breathe (1 minute).** Sit tall and take slow breaths, longer on the out-breath.
2. **Shoulder rolls (1 minute).** Eight in each direction.
3. **Neck side stretch (1 minute).** Three to five breaths each side.
4. **Chin tucks (1 minute).** Five slow repeats.
5. **Seated cat-cow (1 minute).** Six to eight breaths.
6. **Seated spinal twist (1 minute).** Three breaths each side, twice.
7. **Eagle arms (1 minute).** Three to five breaths each side.
8. **Standing forward fold with a chair (1 minute).** Five slow breaths.
9. **Chest opener at the wall (1 minute).** Three breaths each side.
10. **Stand and breathe (1 minute).** Feel your feet on the floor and let your arms hang heavy.

## Small desk habits that help

- **Take a break every 30 to 45 minutes.** Stand, walk to the water cooler, or look out of the window.
- **Raise your screen to eye level.** A stack of books or a laptop stand can help.
- **Keep your feet on the floor.** If they don't reach, use a footrest.
- **Switch position.** Alternate between sitting, standing and walking when you can.
- **Notice your jaw and shoulders.** Drop them a few times each hour.
- **Set a reminder.** A phone alarm every hour is a simple nudge to move.

## When to see a doctor

Stiffness from sitting is common, but some symptoms need a medical opinion. Please speak to your doctor if you notice:

- Pain that's sharp, severe or getting worse
- Numbness, tingling or weakness in your arms or hands
- Pain that spreads down your arm or leg
- Headaches that keep returning
- Pain after an injury or fall

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should I stretch at my desk?

A short stretch every hour or so is a good aim. If that feels like too much, begin with two or three breaks across the day and add more when it becomes a habit.

### Will yoga improve my posture?

Yoga isn't a quick change, but regular practice can help you notice how you sit and stand, and build the strength and flexibility that make good posture easier to hold. Be patient and keep going.

### Can I do these stretches in work clothes?

Yes. Most of these are chair-based or wall-based and need no mat. If you wear a tight skirt or formal trousers, choose the neck, shoulder and arm stretches.
