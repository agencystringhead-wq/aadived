The hardest part of your first yoga class is usually the doorway. You're wondering whether you're flexible enough, whether everyone else will know what to do, and whether you'll be the one who wobbles. Here's the honest version: nobody expects you to be good at this, and a decent beginner class is built around that.

This guide walks you through the whole thing, from what to wear to what the teacher will say in the first ten minutes, so the room feels familiar before you step into it.

## Before you go: a five-point checklist

Most first-class nerves come from small unknowns. Clear these up the day before and you can turn up relaxed.

1. **Wear what you can move in.** Stretchy leggings or shorts, and a top that stays put when you fold forward. Skip jeans, belts and anything stiff.
2. **Eat lightly.** A heavy meal feels uncomfortable in forward folds and twists. Many people prefer a small snack an hour or two before class, or nothing heavy at all.
3. **Ask about the mat.** Places differ on whether they lend mats. Ask us when you book, so you aren't carrying one you didn't need to.
4. **Arrive ten minutes early.** You'll have time to find your way around, change if you need to, and say hello to the teacher.
5. **Mention anything that's going on in your body.** A sore knee, a stiff lower back, a recent injury, pregnancy. A good teacher will offer you a gentler version of a pose.

If you're pregnant or managing a health condition, please speak to your doctor before your first class.

## What happens in the first ten minutes

Most beginner classes follow a similar shape: settle, warm up, move through a few standing and floor poses, cool down, then rest. At AadiVed, every class blends asana, breathwork and grounding practices, so the breath is part of the class from the very first minute.

### Settling in

You'll usually begin seated or lying down. The teacher might ask you to close your eyes, or just soften your gaze, and take a few slow breaths. This isn't a test. If your mind is racing about work or the traffic on the way in, that's normal, and it's exactly why the settling time exists.

### Warming up

Expect gentle spinal movements and a few simple stretches. Your joints and muscles are getting ready, so keep everything small and slow. If you can talk in full sentences, you're working at the right level.

## The poses you're likely to meet

You won't need to remember any Sanskrit names. Here are the shapes beginners meet most often, and how each one should feel.

- **Child's pose (Balasana).** Kneel, sit back towards your heels and fold forward, with your arms long or resting by your sides. It's a resting pose, so you can come back to it at any point in class. It should feel calm, not strained.
- **Cat-cow.** On hands and knees, breathe in as you let your belly drop and your chest lift, then breathe out as you round your back. Six to eight slow rounds is typical. It should feel like a gentle wave through your spine.
- **Downward-facing dog.** From hands and knees, lift your hips up and back. Bend your knees as much as you like, and press the floor away with your hands. If your wrists complain, ask for a forearm variation.
- **Mountain pose and standing forward fold.** Stand with your feet about hip-width apart. When you fold forward, keep your knees soft. You don't need to touch the floor.
- **Low lunge and warrior II.** These build steadiness in the legs. Your front knee should stay over your ankle, not pushing past your toes.
- **Final rest (Savasana).** You lie on your back for a few minutes and let everything go quiet. Many first-timers find this the most surprising part, because stopping is harder than moving.

## How a first class feels, and what's normal

Every body responds differently, but a few things come up again and again.

- You may feel muscles you'd forgotten about, especially in the hips, hamstrings and shoulders.
- Holding a pose can make your legs shake a little. That usually means the muscles are working, not that something is wrong.
- You might feel a bit stiff the next day. A gentle walk may help you feel looser.
- Looking around the room is fine. Everyone does it, and most people are busy thinking about their own balance.

What isn't normal is sharp, pinching or joint pain. If you feel that, ease out of the pose and tell the teacher. The same goes for dizziness: come down slowly and breathe.

### A two-minute warm-up to try tonight

If you'd like a head start, this short sequence uses the same shapes you'll meet in class. Do it on a mat or a folded blanket.

1. **Cat-cow, six breaths.** Move slowly, matching each movement to one breath.
2. **Child's pose, five slow breaths.** Let your forehead rest on the floor or a cushion.
3. **Standing forward fold with soft knees, five breaths.** Let your head and arms hang heavy.
4. **Lie on your back for one minute.** Notice your breathing without trying to change it.

## If something doesn't feel right

You're always allowed to change what you're doing. In a good class, that isn't a failure, it's part of the practice.

- Come back to child's pose whenever you need a rest.
- Bend your knees, use a block or a cushion, or shorten your stance.
- Ask the teacher for another option. Most teachers love being asked.
- Stop completely if anything hurts, and speak to your doctor if the pain stays.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need to be flexible?

No. Flexibility is something yoga builds over time, it isn't a requirement for starting. Poses can be adjusted to suit stiff hips, tight shoulders or a stiff back, and a good teacher will show you how.

### How many classes before it feels easier?

It varies from person to person. Many people find the first class the most unfamiliar, and feel more at ease once they know the rhythm of a class. Going at a pace that feels kind to your body matters more than counting sessions.

### Is online yoga easier to start with?

Some people prefer to begin at home, where nobody's watching and the mat is already laid out. Live online classes still give you real-time cues and corrections. If you're curious, have a look at our [live online classes](/online-classes/) and see which feels more like you.
