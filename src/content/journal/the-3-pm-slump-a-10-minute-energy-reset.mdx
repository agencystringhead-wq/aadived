It's three in the afternoon. Lunch is a distant memory, your eyes are heavy, and the document in front of you has stopped making sense. You reach for another cup of chai, or you scroll your phone. Sound familiar?

The afternoon slump is common, and a short, kind reset often does more than another cup of caffeine. This ten-minute sequence uses movement and breath to wake you up gently. You don't need a mat or a change of clothes, just a chair and a bit of space.

## Why the afternoon slump happens

There's no single cause, and it varies from person to person. A few things that often play a part:

- **Sitting for hours.** Staying still can leave you feeling heavy and stiff.
- **A big or sugary lunch.** Meals can make some people sleepy afterwards.
- **Not enough water.** Mild dehydration can leave you feeling tired.
- **Poor sleep.** A short night makes the afternoon harder.
- **Mental fatigue.** Hours of concentration can leave your attention frayed.
- **Stress.** Tension can drain energy as well as raise it.

If you're very tired every day, or tired despite enough sleep, please speak to your doctor, as persistent tiredness can have medical causes.

## What helps

The idea behind a reset is simple: change your position, move your spine, breathe more fully and give your eyes a break. You aren't trying to force energy. You're giving your body a reason to feel awake.

<PullQuote>You don't have to power through the slump. A few minutes of movement and breath can be a kinder way out of it.</PullQuote>

## The 10-minute reset

Do these in order. Move slowly, and breathe through your nose unless told otherwise.

1. **Stand and shake out (1 minute).** Stand up and let your arms, hands and legs shake loosely. Roll your shoulders and wiggle your fingers.
2. **Reach and sway (1 minute).** Reach both arms overhead and clasp your hands. Lean gently to the right for three breaths, then to the left. Keep your feet planted.
3. **Standing forward fold (1 minute).** Fold forward with soft knees and let your head hang. Nod and shake your head slowly. Roll up slowly, one vertebra at a time.
4. **Chair cat-cow (1 minute).** Sit on the front half of your chair with your hands on your knees. Breathe in as you lift your chest, and breathe out as you round your back. Repeat eight times.
5. **Seated twist (1 minute).** Turn gently to the right, holding the back of your chair, for three breaths. Switch sides.
6. **Eye rest (1 minute).** Rub your palms together until warm. Close your eyes and rest your palms lightly over them. Breathe slowly. When you open your eyes, look at something far away for ten seconds.
7. **Energising breath (2 minutes).** Sit tall. Breathe in through your nose for a count of four, and out through your nose for a count of four. Try to keep the breath smooth. After a minute, try breathing in for four and out for six.
8. **Stand tall and set an intention (2 minutes).** Stand with your feet hip-width apart. Press into your feet, lengthen through the crown of your head and take three slow breaths. Choose one thing you'll do next.

Drink some water when you finish.

## If you only have two minutes

Pick this short version.

1. Stand up and reach your arms overhead for three breaths.
2. Fold forward with soft knees for three breaths.
3. Sit tall and take five slow breaths, four in, four out.

Even this tiny reset can make the rest of the afternoon feel different.

## A word of caution on fast breathing

You may see "energising" breathing practices that involve fast, forceful breaths. These can cause dizziness, especially for beginners, and they're not suitable for everyone, including people who are pregnant or who have heart, lung or blood pressure conditions. The reset above sticks to smooth, easy breathing. If you'd like to try more advanced techniques, please learn them with a teacher and check with your doctor first. ## Other small things that help

- **Drink water.** Keep a bottle on your desk and sip through the afternoon.
- **Move around.** A short walk, even to the stairs and back, can help.
- **Get daylight.** A few minutes by a window or outside can lift your mood.
- **Eat a lighter lunch.** If heavy meals make you sleepy, try a smaller portion with some protein and vegetables.
- **Plan tasks sensibly.** Save lighter tasks for the afternoon, if you can.
- **Protect your sleep.** An earlier night is often the best slump remedy.

If your neck and shoulders ache as the day goes on, <PostLink slug="yoga-for-desk-workers-easing-neck-shoulder-and-back-stiffness">yoga for desk workers</PostLink> has stretches you can do in your chair.

## Building it into your day

A reset works best when it's a habit, not a rescue. A few ways to make it stick:

- **Set a daily alarm.** Pick a time, maybe 3 pm, and treat it like a meeting.
- **Pair it with something.** Do it after your afternoon tea or before a certain call.
- **Invite a colleague.** A shared break is easier to keep.
- **Keep it short.** Two minutes counts.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I do this in office clothes?

Yes. Everything here works in regular clothes. If your outfit is tight, skip the forward fold and twist, and focus on the reaches, eye rest and breath.

### How often should I do it?

Once a day is a good aim, at whatever time you tend to slump. You can also use it any time you notice your focus slipping.

### Will it replace my chai or coffee?

It doesn't need to. Many people enjoy both. Try the reset first and see whether you still feel you need the extra cup.

Next time the afternoon drags, stand up before you reach for your phone. Ten minutes might be all you need.
