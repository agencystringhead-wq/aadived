You're in the middle of a meeting, and you realise you've read the same line three times. Or you're lying in bed, and your mind is rehearsing tomorrow. Your body is in one place and your attention is somewhere else entirely. That scattered feeling is common, and the simplest way back is to turn your attention to what's here right now.

That's what grounding practices do. They're short, quiet exercises that use your senses, your breath and your body to bring you back to the present moment. You can do them anywhere, with no equipment at all.

## What grounding means

In yoga, grounding means connecting with the earth beneath you and the body you're in. It's often the opposite of the "up in the head" feeling, where thoughts race and the body feels distant.

Grounding practices work by giving your attention a simple, solid place to land: the feeling of your feet on the floor, the sound in the room, the weight of your hands. They're ordinary and quiet, and they don't ask you to change how you feel. You only notice what's already here.

<Note title="A note on grounding">

Grounding practices are a supportive habit, not a treatment. If you often feel overwhelmed, panicky or disconnected, please speak to your doctor or a mental health professional. This article is for general information.

</Note>

## When grounding can help

You might reach for a grounding practice when:

- Your thoughts are racing and you can't switch off
- You've just had a stressful call or an argument
- You feel scattered between tasks
- You're lying awake with a busy mind
- You're about to do something that makes you nervous

You can also use these practices as a daily habit, even when you feel fine, so they're familiar when you need them.

## Five practices to try

### 1. The 5-4-3-2-1 check

This is a well-known way to bring your attention to the senses. Look around slowly and name, silently or out loud:

- **5 things you can see.** The colour of a wall, a plant, your hands.
- **4 things you can feel.** The chair under you, your clothes, the air on your skin.
- **3 things you can hear.** A fan, distant traffic, your own breath.
- **2 things you can smell.** Tea, soap, fresh air. If nothing, imagine a smell you like.
- **1 thing you can taste.** A sip of water, or the taste in your mouth.

Take your time. The point isn't to finish quickly, it's to notice.

### 2. Feet on the floor

Sit or stand with both feet flat. Press them gently into the floor and notice the contact: the heels, the balls of the feet, the toes. Feel the floor supporting you. Take five slow breaths, imagining each out-breath settling into the ground beneath you. If you're sitting, notice the weight of your body on the chair too.

### 3. Barefoot standing

If you can, stand barefoot on grass, soil, sand or a cool floor. Spread your toes, and feel the texture and temperature beneath you. Shift your weight slowly from one foot to the other. Stay for a few minutes and notice the sounds and smells around you.

Many people enjoy this early in the morning or after work, when stepping outside feels like a pause. Check the ground for anything sharp, and skip this if you have a foot condition or reduced feeling in your feet.

### 4. Hands and warmth

Rub your palms together until they feel warm. Rest them on your chest, belly or closed eyes, and feel the gentle warmth. Breathe slowly for eight to ten breaths. This small, kind gesture can be done at your desk, in a queue or before sleep.

### 5. The long exhale

Breathe in gently for a count of four, and breathe out for a count of six. Let your shoulders drop with each exhale. Repeat for one to two minutes. ## A two-minute grounding routine

Use this when you've only got a short time.

1. **Feel your feet (30 seconds).** Press them into the floor. Notice the contact.
2. **Look around (30 seconds).** Name three things you can see, slowly.
3. **Listen (30 seconds).** Notice three sounds, near and far.
4. **Breathe (30 seconds).** Take four slow breaths, longer on the exhale.

Finish by asking yourself one question: what's the next small thing I need to do? Then do that one thing.

## Grounding in yoga

Many yoga poses are grounding by design. They're often low, steady and focused on the feet, legs and hips.

- **Mountain pose.** Stand tall, spread your toes and feel the floor under all four corners of each foot.
- **Child's pose.** Rest your forehead on the mat and feel the support of the ground.
- **Easy seated pose with hands on knees.** Notice your sit bones and the weight of your body.
- **Standing forward fold.** Let your head and arms hang, feeling gravity pull gently.
- **Lying rest.** Let the floor hold you completely.

## Making grounding a habit

The more familiar these practices are, the easier they are to reach for when things feel tricky.

- **Pick one cue.** Ground yourself each time you sit at your desk or step through your front door.
- **Practise when you feel fine.** A calm minute each morning builds the habit.
- **Keep it small.** A single breath with your feet on the floor counts.
- **Use a reminder.** A note on your desk or a phone alarm can nudge you.
- **Be patient.** It's normal for your mind to wander. Gently return to your feet or breath.

## When your feelings are strong

Grounding can help you pause, but it may not make big feelings go away, and that's okay. If you feel very anxious, panicky, or numb for long periods, please reach out to your doctor or a mental health professional. You don't have to manage alone. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long should I practise grounding?

Even thirty seconds can help. Many people use a two-minute routine, and some like a longer sit of five to ten minutes. Choose what fits your day.

### Do I need to be barefoot?

No. You can feel your feet in socks or shoes too. Barefoot is an option for those who enjoy it, and the other practices work just as well anywhere.

### Is grounding the same as meditation?

They're related. Grounding is usually shorter and more focused on the senses and the body. Meditation often lasts longer and involves resting your attention on one thing. They work well together.
